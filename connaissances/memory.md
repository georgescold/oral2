**Key theoretical framework (used consistently across work):**
- **ERPI filter** (Catteau, 1970): Équilibration, Respiration, Propulsion, Information — used as the primary qualitative lens; qualitative analysis must be justified by quantitative data, not separated from it
- **Indices de nage** (Costill, 1985): amplitude × fréquence = vitesse
- **Newell (1986)**: dispositif design (task/environment/organism constraints)
- **Schmidt (1975)**: PMG (programme moteur généralisé), re-parameterization, feedback loops
- **Bernstein (1967)**: progressive degree-of-freedom liberation
- **Seifert & Chollet (2007)**: coordination types in breaststroke (glissant, continu, chevauchement)
- Researchers regularly referenced: Pelayo, Maglischo, Chollet

**Oral structure (strict 6-step methodology):**
1. Contextual introduction + targeted competency area (CA)
2. Video analysis via ERPI filter with quantitative data
3. Three hypotheses (cognitive, emotional, physiological/biomechanical) — cognitive typically primary
4. Transformation axis with behavioral progression
5. Evolutionary situation with full pedagogical scaffolding (three levels)
6. Short/medium/long-term conclusion tied to target CA

**Key learnings & principles**

- **Qualitative must be grounded in quantitative:** Don't separate the two analyses — justify qualitative observations through the numbers.
- **Hypothesis hierarchy matters:** Cognitive hypothesis is typically primary; physiological/biomechanical and emotional are secondary; fatigue is often a consequence, not a cause.
- **Realistic target-setting:** Baseline metrics from near-sprint or atypical conditions cannot serve as N3 targets — adjust to realistic performance expectations.
- **Coulée exclusion:** In distance events, the coulée phase must be excluded from stroke analysis calculations.
- **Pull-buoy as constraint, not aid:** Used specifically to isolate upper body mechanics and correct coordination faults (e.g., asymmetric kick, Plié-Écarté-Serré in breaststroke).
- **Chevauchement coordination** is most efficient for breaststroke sprint (eliminates motor gap) but energetically costly — not appropriate for all levels.
- **Dispositif simplicity:** No unnecessary equipment (e.g., no planche — bras en flèche instead); constraints should be purposeful and theoretically justified.
- **Jury anticipation:** Prepare concise two-sentence answers for each of the four oral moments.
